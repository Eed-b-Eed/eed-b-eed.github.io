Khalili Team Members

At the heart of Eed-b-Eed is the family collective of five siblings from al-Khalil (Hebron). These are four sisters – A. Hroub, B. Hroub, R. Hroub, and S. Hroub – and their brother Ahmad Hroub.

They come from rural al-Khalil, and currently live across the Bethlehem and al-Khalil regions. Proud speakers of Khalili Arabic in its rural form, they are passionate about making their language thrive, amidst the linguistic pressures related to urbanisation, electronic communication, and AI, all of which lead to the gradual disappearance of local language varieties, and, subsequently, of the cultural traditions they carry.

All five siblings hold degrees from Palestinian universities, including Birzeit University, in fields including Arabic language and Literature, Law, and Economics.

For the past four years the siblings have been working as an institutionally contracted team in research projects led by Dr Jakub Zbrzezny at the University of Aberdeen, enabled by internal university funding and external grants from academic institutions such as the British Academy, Royal Society of Edinburgh, or Cornell University’s Department of Near Eastern Studies. Their work concerns pre-modern Arabic translations of Jewish and Christian Scriptures and their contemporary understanding among Muslim communities. You will see Ahmad co-presenting with Jakub at conferences and seminars, in person or online.

Building on Ahmad’s extensive experience in privately teaching Khalili Arabic, the siblings have started to develop didactic materials for Eed-b-Eed, thanks to grants from the Knowledge Exchange and Innovation Fund of the Scottish Funding Council and from the UK-based Al Tajir Trust, both administered by the University of Aberdeen. In due course, Eed-b-Eed learners will meet the siblings during live practice sessions. In future, the Hroub sisters will facilitate the recruitment of further local team members from among university-educated local women, and Ahmad’s training in economics will significantly help in making this model sustainable in the local ecosystem.

Aberdonian Team Members

Eed-b-Eed is academically coordinated by Dr Jakub Zbrzezny, lecturer in the School of Divinity, History, Philosophy and Art History at the University of Aberdeen (UoA) in Scotland.

In addition to his teaching and research on the ancient “Holy Land” and its languages, Jakub has, for the past four years, been coordinating the University’s distinctive online programme in theology. Many of its 80 students are professionals who combine part-time study with full-time work. Blending guided self-study with live evening tutorials, the programme offers flexibility without compromising learning quality. This is what Jakub wants to give to online Eed-b-Eed learners.

Originally from Poland, Jakub completed Interdisciplinary Individual Studies in the Humanities at the University of Warsaw, and later gained a PhD in Divinity at the University of Aberdeen. In his doctoral research he investigated correlations between early textual traditions of the Jewish, Christian, and Muslim Scriptures. In his current research, he explores how the traditional knowledge and cultural intuitions of rural Arab communities in Palestine can shed new light on ancient biblical traditions associated with the region. In line with the University of Aberdeen’s ethical principle of ‘Sustainable partnerships with stakeholders’ expressed in its Institutional Ethical Statement, Jakub’s Palestinian research partners receive for their intellectual work fair and appropriate remuneration at a level comparable to that of University research assistants.

Jakub’s long-standing passion for the Scriptures led him first to Latin and Greek, then to Hebrew and Syriac, and ultimately to Arabic, in both its ancient and modern manifestations. Thanks to the linguistic hospitality of Ahmad Hroub, Jakub speaks Khalili Arabic and is passionate about its study. Indeed, he strongly believes that research on the “Holy Land” in antiquity should be attentive to the lives of its modern inhabitants across Israel and Palestine, Jews and Arabs alike.

International Team Members

International friends of Eed-b-Eed contribute expertise essential for its development.

Bringing a wealth of experience from her educational work in India, followed by a Master’s degree at the Harvard Graduate School of Education, Sapna Saleem helps us to ensure that Eed-b-Eed is accessible to learners from a variety of backgrounds across the world.

Originally from India, Sapna spent most of her early years in Bahrain. Although trained to be an engineer, her interest in the field of education and belief in the collective work of social development led her to become an educator. Sapna's passion for how to make learning engaging has led her to work as a teacher and curriculum designer. Her experience teaching and designing learning programs has allowed her to work with young kids in primary schools to adult learners in universities.

She began teaching in low-resourced communities in India, designed education interventions for programs in Kenya and Sierra Leone and most recently taught graduate students and executive leaders at Harvard University.

As a Harvard student, Sapna had the opportunity to visit Palestine and Israel that contributes to her interest in livelihood development for young women in the region.

Behind the scenes

International academic projects such as Eed-b-Eed are often associated with the researchers who lead or coordinate them. However, they would not be possible without the behind-the-scenes work of many colleagues from Professional Services across the University’s administration. Their efforts and professionalism are greatly appreciated both in Aberdeen and in al-Khalil.

Eed-b-Eed respects individual preferences regarding public photographs. Some team members therefore choose to be represented by illustrations rather than portraits.
